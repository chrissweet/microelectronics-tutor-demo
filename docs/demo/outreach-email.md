Subject: SCALE and AI — a small test of "AI that knows the course"

Hi Peter and Kerrie,

Apologies for how late this is. I have been thinking about SCALE and AI and what the path forward looks like, and the honest summary is that I think the biggest challenge we face as educators is not AI itself, it's the misuse of AI. Students are using it in assignments and in their general approach to learning, and we should not try to prevent this from happening. AI-detector tools strike me as the wrong fix. Some students are using AI responsibly already and it is helping them; my hunch is that the students using it irresponsibly are exactly the ones who would benefit most from being tutored through the problem rather than caught and penalized for it.

What I think we should do instead is embrace AI, including the student's choice of AI, and give it the resources it needs to tutor the student rather than just hand over the answers. As a test I took one of the courses listed under SCALE on nanoHub, the Introduction to Engineering with Microelectronics curriculum (https://www.scale4me.org/scale-curriculum/introduction-to-engineering-with-microelectronics-curriculum-plan), and built a small course-specific wiki from it. I then attached that wiki to an AI session along with some pedagogical guardrails (Socratic prompting, no complete solutions, that sort of thing).

I made two short videos. Both use the same underlying AI (Claude Code). The only difference is what it can see: in Video 1 it has no course context and just answers Lab 1 directly; in Video 2 it is grounded in the course wiki and walks the student through the reasoning. The same diagnostic question appears at the end of each, and the cheat-path student cannot reason through it while the tutor-path student can. That is the whole pitch in one beat.

If you have around 5 minutes to watch both, I would love your reactions. And if you see something worth pursuing, I would happily take 15 minutes to talk through what a SCALE pilot could look like.

(One side note for that discussion if it comes up: I am cautious about chasing things like edge-AI workforce training, given that industry already leads there, but happy to be wrong on that.)

Best,
Chris
